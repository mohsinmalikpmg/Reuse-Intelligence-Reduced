You are right. We have **overfit the product story to one seeded maintenance scenario**.

That was useful for making the A/B workbench concrete and deterministic, but it created an unintended implication:

> Fusebox looks like an operational-maintenance application rather than an enterprise platform for discovering, testing and consolidating solutions across Cenovus.

Nothing in the original problem statement limits the challenge to field operations or manufacturing workflows. The problem is enterprise-wide: AI-enabled teams can independently build overlapping solutions to the same business problem in **any function**.

## Why the operational bias appeared

Four design choices created it:

1. **Maintenance work orders were selected as the hero scenario.** They provide clear inputs, workflow steps, recurring patterns and measurable outputs.

2. **The current execution engine is built around work-order data.** This made operations the easiest place to demonstrate genuine A/B functionality.

3. **The Fusebox electrical identity was combined with refinery imagery.** That visually reinforced “industrial operations tool,” even though electrical convergence is intended as a metaphor for enterprise solution consolidation.

4. **We defined personas by platform role but demonstrated them through one operational context.** `Find`, `Own` and `Govern` are valid platform roles, but the content beneath them was almost entirely maintenance-oriented.

That narrowness was a demo implementation decision, not the right long-term product definition.

# The correct product position

Fusebox should be framed as:

> **Cenovus’s enterprise solution discovery, experimentation and consolidation platform.**

It should support:

- Operational workflows
- Finance and management reporting
- Investor relations and corporate reporting
- ESG and regulatory reporting
- Supply chain
- Planning and scheduling
- Commercial analysis
- Resource management
- Procurement
- Legal and contract review
- HR and workforce processes
- Executive decision support

The common problem is not the function. The common problem is:

> Multiple teams create overlapping approaches, and Cenovus needs to find, compare, improve and consolidate them.

# A broader solution landscape

The Library should demonstrate several solution families, even though only one or two need to be fully executable for the hackathon.

| Business function | Example problem | Example Fusebox solution family | Typical output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finance | Management reporting takes too long and commentary is inconsistent | **Management Performance Pack** | Reconciled results, variance commentary and exception summary |
| Investor relations | Drafting and proofing outlook materials requires repeated manual review | **Disclosure Draft & Proof** | Source-linked first draft, consistency findings and review flags |
| ESG / Regulatory | Evidence is fragmented across systems and disclosure owners | **ESG Evidence Hub** | Disclosure coverage, supporting evidence and unresolved gaps |
| Planning | Plans are built in separate tools with conflicting assumptions | **Integrated Planning Assistant** | Feasible schedule, constraint analysis and scenario comparison |
| Supply chain | Inventory, purchasing and operational demand are not consistently aligned | **Supply Chain Optimizer** | Recommended order plan, shortages, excess and supplier risks |
| Resource management | Energy, water or emissions usage is difficult to monitor and explain | **Resource Intelligence** | Anomalies, forecasts and intervention recommendations |
| Commercial / Strategy | Analysts repeatedly construct similar scenarios and decision packs | **Scenario Decision Studio** | Assumptions, sensitivities, alternatives and recommendation |
| Procurement / Legal | Contracts require repetitive extraction and risk review | **Contract Review Workbench** | Clause summary, deviations, risks and approval routing |
| Executive reporting | Leaders receive large volumes of inconsistent information | **Executive Briefing Builder** | Concise sourced briefing, key decisions and emerging risks |
| Operations | Work-order information is reviewed manually and inconsistently | **Work Order Intelligence** | Recurring issues, safety signals and recommended actions |

This would make it clear that maintenance is **one circuit within Fusebox**, not Fusebox itself.

# How the platform remains coherent across functions

The product should not contain a different architecture for every department. It should have a common set of reusable capability types:

1. **Ingest** — retrieve documents, records, reports or transactions.
2. **Extract** — identify entities, values, clauses, issues or events.
3. **Validate** — reconcile numbers, verify completeness and detect contradictions.
4. **Analyze** — classify, cluster, forecast, optimize or compare.
5. **Draft** — produce summaries, reports, disclosures or recommendations.
6. **Review** — expose sources, exceptions, changes and confidence.
7. **Approve** — route decisions through accountable owners.
8. **Monitor** — track adoption, outcomes, controls and performance.

The same capability may support several business functions.

For example:

- A **source-grounded summarizer** can support work orders, investor materials and executive briefings.
- An **evidence validator** can support ESG disclosures, financial reporting and regulatory submissions.
- A **constraint analyzer** can support maintenance scheduling, supply planning and workforce allocation.
- An **approval workflow** can support contract review, management reports and operational changes.

That reuse across functions is exactly the organizational value Fusebox should make visible.

# The schematic Library should reflect the enterprise

The electrical schematic metaphor becomes more useful when it is not tied only to operations.

A strong schematic hierarchy would be:

**Enterprise function → Business problem → Solution family → Workflow → Reusable capability → Output**

Visually:

- **Busbars** represent Finance, Operations, ESG, Supply Chain, Commercial and Corporate.
- **Circuit branches** represent business problems.
- **Fuse panels** represent governed solution families.
- **Components** represent reusable capabilities.
- **Output terminals** represent reports, recommendations, schedules, alerts or decisions.
- **Illuminated shared circuits** reveal that one capability is reused across several functions.

A user could click `Evidence Validator` and see it illuminate connections to:

- ESG Evidence Hub
- Management Performance Pack
- Disclosure Draft & Proof
- Contract Review Workbench

That communicates enterprise reuse more powerfully than a list of component names.

# The output comparison must adapt to the type of work

The A/B workbench should not assume every output is a block of maintenance text.

It needs several output modes.

## Document and disclosure comparison

For investor, regulatory or ESG reporting:

- Side-by-side drafts
- Changed claims
- Source coverage
- Contradictions
- Missing evidence
- Unsupported statements
- Reviewer comments

The key question becomes:

> Which version is more complete, accurate, supportable and review-ready?

## Financial and management reporting comparison

For performance packs:

- Reconciled versus unreconciled values
- Variance explanations
- Missing commentary
- Exceptions
- Source traceability
- Time required to complete the pack

The key question becomes:

> Which workflow produces a more accurate and decision-useful report with fewer manual interventions?

## Planning and scheduling comparison

For planning workflows:

- Schedule feasibility
- Constraint violations
- Resource utilization
- Service level
- Cost
- Delays
- Scenario sensitivity

The key question becomes:

> Which plan better balances operational, resource and financial constraints?

## Monitoring comparison

For resource usage or supply-chain monitoring:

- Alerts identified
- False positives
- Missed anomalies
- Time to detection
- Recommended response
- Expected operational effect

## Decision-support comparison

For an executive briefing or scenario analysis:

- Decision completeness
- Assumptions
- Evidence
- Alternatives considered
- Risks
- Recommendation clarity

This broadens the secret sauce from “compare two generated texts” to:

> **Compare how different solution configurations affect the actual business output.**

# Financial, investor and regulatory use cases require stronger controls

These are valuable Fusebox use cases, but they need different governance from a maintenance prototype.

For investor reports, financial reporting and regulatory disclosures, Fusebox should support:

- Source-linked claims
- Data lineage
- Version control
- Segregation of duties
- Reviewer and approver roles
- Immutable evidence records
- Change tracking
- Clear distinction between draft and approved output
- No autonomous external publication

The platform can draft, reconcile, flag and proof. It should not independently authorize public financial or regulatory disclosure.

This actually strengthens the business case: Fusebox is not just combining features; it is preserving the correct controls for each type of solution.

# Persona model: use two dimensions

The earlier persona model only described how people interact with the Fusebox lifecycle:

- Find
- Own
- Govern

That remains useful. But we also need business personas.

## Platform role

- **Solution seeker:** finds and tries what exists.
- **Solution owner:** manages and improves a governed solution.
- **Technology owner:** governs architecture, support and consolidation.

## Business role

- **Analyst or knowledge worker:** prepares analysis, reports or recommendations.
- **Manager or process owner:** reviews outputs and controls the workflow.
- **Executive or decision-maker:** consumes trusted decision outputs.
- **Specialist or operator:** performs domain-specific work.

This avoids creating a separate application for Finance, ESG and Operations.

A financial analyst and maintenance analyst may both use `Find`, but they search different business problems and receive different output formats.

# What an executive experience should be

Executives generally should not enter the workflow builder.

They need a concise **Decision View** generated from the same evidence:

- Business problem
- Existing overlapping solutions
- Recommended base product
- Proposed improvement
- A/B evidence
- Risks and controls
- Expected value
- Owner recommendation
- Decision required

For a portfolio-level executive view, show:

- Business problems with multiple active solutions
- Reuse before build
- Improvements folded into existing products
- Products awaiting retirement
- Investment or cost exposure
- High-risk unowned solutions
- Decisions requiring executive sponsorship

This should be a read-oriented view, not another configuration dashboard.

# Visual identity also needs broadening

The Power Cube and electrical schematic remain appropriate. The heavy refinery imagery should become a contextual accent rather than the defining visual.

Recommended change:

- Make the **Power Cube and enterprise circuitry** the main product imagery.
- Use refinery, office, planning, finance and field imagery selectively by use case.
- Avoid making every hero view look like a control room.
- Show multiple enterprise circuits flowing into the Power Cube.
- Use output-specific icons: report, chart, calendar, contract, disclosure, alert, decision and work order.

The product should feel born from an energy company, but not limited to physical energy operations.

Add representative solution families across at least:

- Finance
- ESG / Regulatory
- Supply Chain / Planning
- Corporate / Executive Reporting
- Operations

These do not all need working execution engines. They need credible metadata, workflows, capabilities, ownership and output previews.

Each Library record should include:

- Business function
- Business problem
- Primary user
- Workflow type
- Output type
- Risk tier
- Data involved
- Controls
- Owner
- Maturity

### 3. Reframe the hero scenario

Keep Work Order Intelligence as the deeply functioning example, but explicitly introduce it as:

> One example of Fusebox’s enterprise consolidation model.

The opening Library or schematic should show several business functions before entering the maintenance demonstration.

### 4. Add one credible back-office preview

The lowest-effort, highest-value second example would likely be:

**Management Performance Pack**

Input:

- Monthly financial data
- Operational KPIs
- Prior-period commentary

Output:

- Variance summary
- Draft management commentary
- Exceptions requiring review
- Source references

It can initially be a deterministic preview rather than a fully developed engine.

### 5. Add an executive Decision View

After the A/B test, provide a compact decision summary that could apply to either an operational or back-office solution.

# What not to do before Thursday

Do not try to implement ten fully working domain workflows.

The right hackathon balance is:

> **One deeply functional use case, one credible secondary use case, and a Library architecture that visibly supports the rest of the enterprise.**

That demonstrates both depth and scalability.

Eight shallow demos would be less convincing than:

- One real same-input A/B workflow
- One back-office output preview
- One enterprise-wide solution schematic
- One executive consolidation view

# How this changes the judging framework

The rubric should add explicit enterprise applicability.

Under **Problem Fidelity and Business Value**, judges should assess whether the solution:

- Works beyond one operational use case
- Uses a domain-neutral solution and capability model
- Demonstrates reuse across functions
- Supports different output types
- Applies controls appropriate to higher-risk business processes

Under **Governance and Scalability**, judges should assess whether:

- The same platform model can support operations, finance, ESG and planning
- Capabilities can be reused across domains
- Business-specific configurations remain under one governed solution family
- Executive decisions remain traceable to actual evidence

## Revised strategic statement

The platform should no longer be described as:

> A way to combine maintenance or operational solutions.

It should be described as:

> **An enterprise platform that helps Cenovus find overlapping solutions, reuse common capabilities, prove improvements against real business outputs, and converge on one governed product—across operations, corporate functions and executive decision-making.**

The operations workflow remains an effective demonstration. It should not define the boundary of the product.
